A number is not a finding until you say what it is being compared against

Every statistic on this page is computed from real annotated inter-click intervals — no synthesis, no model. What changes between the panels below is not the data and not the statistic. It is the null model: the specific thing you are claiming the effect is not. Switch it and watch conclusions that looked solid dissolve, or survive.

The headline number to watch is explained by null — how much of the observed value the control already produces on its own. A vanishing p-value sitting next to “the null explains 99.0% of this” is not a discovery, and this tool is built to make that visible rather than let it hide behind significance.

But it is not a single scale. Each null controls for a different thing, so their percentages answer different questions and cannot be composed. The clan claim's stratified null controls for repertoire composition but not for the fact that codas from one social unit are not independent; its cluster null does the reverse. 99.0% and 25.3% are both true and neither is “the answer”. Where a combined null would be the honest test, the code refuses to fake one rather than print a number that looks like a verdict.

Where coda rhythm sits among other rhythms

nPVI measures durational contrast between adjacent intervals: 0 is a metronome, ~67 is a strict 2:1 alternation, ~100 is a memoryless random process. Every source below is cut into matched 4-interval windows and put through the identical pipeline, because nPVI variance depends strongly on window length.

Not shown, deliberately: published per-language nPVI values for human speech. Those are computed over vocalic intervals across whole sentences, not 4-interval windows, so putting them on this axis would compare measurement protocols rather than rhythms. The drumming comparison is sound because both sides were measured here, the same way.

Benjamini-Hochberg assumes tests are independent or positively dependent. Several of these run on the same codas under different nulls, so they are strongly dependent and BH is not exactly right here. Treat q as an indication of what multiplicity costs you, not as a certified correction — and note that a q-value cannot rescue a result whose null already explains 99% of it.

Your predictions

    What this cannot tell you

    • One population. Every coda here is from clans EC1 and EC2 off Dominica. Nothing generalises to sperm whales elsewhere.
    • Annotation is not raw audio. The intervals were measured by researchers from recordings, with tooling that changed over the 2005–2018 span. This page inherits those decisions and cannot audit them.
    • Rhythm is not meaning. Showing that coda timing carries clan identity says nothing about whether whales intend it, perceive it, or use it referentially.
    • Cross-domain comparison is about form, not function. Whale codas and human drumming occupying different regions of an nPVI axis is a statement about interval timing. It is not evidence that either is or is not language.
    • Observations are not independent, and the tests mostly pretend they are. Codas cluster by individual, by social unit and by recording session; drum windows cluster by take and by drummer (up to 40 windows from one take, 675 takes, ~10 players). A permutation that shuffles individual codas or windows treats correlated observations as independent and overstates precision — which is why the clan claim carries a third null that permutes whole social units, and why its z falls from 71.9 to 4.5 when it does. The stratified-by-coda-type null and the cross-domain claims have not been given clustered versions; read their p-values as upper bounds on certainty, not as measurements of it.
    • Multiple comparisons. Running every claim against every null is many tests. The tool tracks them and reports Benjamini-Hochberg q-values, but those assume independence and several of these tests share the same codas — so treat q as an indication of cost, not a certified correction.
    • Cross-species p-values are close to meaningless. Whale codas and human drumming are not exchangeable under any null, so permuting their labels is guaranteed to produce a tiny p before the data is seen. That comparison is carried by Cohen's d and by the fact that both sides went through an identical pipeline at an identical window length — not by its p-value.
